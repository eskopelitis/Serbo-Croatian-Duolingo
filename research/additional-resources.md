# Additional learning and data resources

Checked 19 November 2026 using the user's local SearXNG and the linked source pages. These resources supplement [the earlier source notes](sources.md). Course availability and licensing may change.

## Courses and tests

- [University of Zagreb A1.HR](https://a1.ffzg.unizg.hr/) and [A2.HR](https://a2.ffzg.unizg.hr/) are Croatian courses linked from [Croatia's government portal](https://gov.hr/en/a1-hr-a2-hr-level-online-croatian-courses-opened/1994). The course pages describe listening, pronunciation, writing, comprehension and grammar, with English and Spanish interfaces. Their login pages say Google or Facebook sign-in is required. They are useful learning links; permission to copy course content into this project has not been established.
- The [Foreign Service Institute Serbo-Croatian Basic Course](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-serbo-croatian-basic-course/) is hosted by a third party and links two scanned student books plus 44 MP3 files. The inspected first volume is a 1965 U.S. State Department textbook covering units 1–25. Its preface says it is based mainly on eastern speech, with some Croatian contributions. The MP3 links on this page are for volume 2, units 26–50; audio for volume 1 was not verified there. The host describes the courses as public domain, but confirm the rights of each specific text and recording before bundling. Terminology and social context are historical; supplement with modern native-speaker review.
- [DLI Serbian Basic Course](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/serbian/courses/dli-serbian-basic-course/) offers downloadable Defense Language Institute materials, including student and instructor documents, through the same third-party site. Treat it as a study reference until the underlying text, audio and third-party materials' rights have been verified individually.
- [EasyBosnian's level test](https://easybosnian.com/cefr-bosnian-test) describes a free placement check and course-specific assessments. The provider says these are its own course assessments, not official CEFR examinations. Independent quality, accessible content and redistribution rights were not verified.
- University of Belgrade's [Serbian language center](https://learnserbian.fil.bg.ac.rs/) publishes sample A1–C2 written and listening material, while the Faculty of Montenegrin Language and Literature hosts its [2018 testing program](https://fcjk.ac.me/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Provjera-znanja-crnogorskoga-jezika-za-strance.pdf). These are distinct institutional resources, not a joint embassy examination. Link to them; a reproduction license was not established.

## Reusable-data candidates

- [UD Serbian SET](https://github.com/UniversalDependencies/UD_Serbian-SET) and [UD Croatian SET](https://github.com/UniversalDependencies/UD_Croatian-SET) provide `.conllu` training, development and test files with sentence text, lemmas and grammar annotation. The maintainers specify CC BY-SA 4.0; the datasets are partially parallel with aligned document splits and are primarily news, not graded English teaching content. Keep the original license, attribution, version and provenance. Annotation is useful for exploring cases, lemmas and Serbian/Croatian contrasts; validate examples before teaching.
- [Serbian Dictionary API](https://github.com/ivkeapp/serbian-dictionary-api) provides a PHP API for words, names and script transliteration. Its repository is GPL-3.0, but its README explicitly says to check individual data-source licenses. Do not assume the code license clears redistribution of every wordlist.
- [sr-sh-nlp](https://github.com/putnich/sr-sh-nlp) contains parsed 2020 Serbo-Croatian Wiktionary data in TEI Lex-0 XML, with synonyms, definitions and some pronunciation. The author describes Wikimedia data as CC BY-SA 3.0; verify the exact underlying dump, attribution and any other dictionary's independent rights before reuse. It is not a current learner-graded vocabulary list.
- [grammCase](https://github.com/madza91/grammCase) is a small PHP grammatical-case project whose repository declares MIT. It has not been audited for linguistic accuracy or suitability for this Python app; any bundled data may have separate provenance.
