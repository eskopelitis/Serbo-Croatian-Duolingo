# Serbo-Croatian research and source notes

Research findings (19 Nov 2026; sources checked through local SearXNG, then original pages and linked primary sources). The default upstreams degraded, but Yandex and Seznam worked through the same local SearXNG; no substitute search service used. Nova owns application changes; findings and follow-up actions were handed off during research.

DATA
• Tatoeba: https://tatoeba.org/en/downloads offers sentence-pair exports and weekly files. Direct detailed sentence downloads verified: https://downloads.tatoeba.org/exports/per_language/srp/srp_sentences_detailed.tsv.bz2 (52,105 rows), https://downloads.tatoeba.org/exports/per_language/hrv/hrv_sentences_detailed.tsv.bz2 (6,582), https://downloads.tatoeba.org/exports/per_language/bos/bos_sentences_detailed.tsv.bz2 (1,759); counts reflect this snapshot and are not guarantees of accuracy, grade or translation availability. https://downloads.tatoeba.org/exports/links.tar.bz2 connects sentence IDs. Site says text exports CC BY 2.0 FR, with some CC0; preserve each sentence ID, author, source URL and license/attribution information for both sides. Contributor audio has separate licenses; site explicitly says audio without a license cannot be reused outside Tatoeba. Check native quality, filter content and verify language/variant. No separately listed Montenegrin category was confirmed.
• FrequencyWords: https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/tree/master/content/2018 has actual sr, hr and bs subtitle-derived 50k and full lists. Verified downloads: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/master/content/2018/sr/sr_50k.txt , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/master/content/2018/hr/hr_50k.txt and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/master/content/2018/bs/bs_50k.txt . Project claims CC BY-SA 4.0 for content and MIT for code; give attribution/share-alike and audit underlying OpenSubtitles rights before shipping. Lists are raw surface-form frequency counts, biased by subtitles, and not CEFR word lists or reliable definitions. Review, lemmatize, de-duplicate scripts, and hand-grade.
• Kaikki/Wiktextract: https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Serbo-Croatian/index.html lists 66,998 forms from an English Wiktionary extraction dated 30 Oct 2026. The 367.8 MB per-language postprocessed JSONL at https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Serbo-Croatian/kaikki.org-dictionary-SerboCroatian.jsonl is explicitly deprecated; the maintained raw English-edition extract is 2.9 GB compressed at https://kaikki.org/dictionary/raw-wiktextract-data.jsonl.gz and must be filtered to Serbo-Croatian. Preserve source entry URLs and Wiktionary attribution/share-alike, review provenance of merged data, disambiguate senses, and manually check usage. Do not equate dictionary size with learner vocabulary.
• Leipzig lists are linked as CC BY 4.0 by https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Frequency_lists/Serbo-Croatian ; its download catalog https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/ served an anti-bot challenge, so exact corpus files, variant and rights remain unverified. Treat as a candidate, not an imported asset.

EXAMS AND LEARNING
• Serbia: University of Belgrade Faculty of Philology's Center for Serbian as a Foreign Language https://learnserbian.fil.bg.ac.rs/ publishes A1–C2 written sample exams, audio and a speaking example; A1 https://learnserbian.fil.bg.ac.rs/files/exam/A1.pdf . Use links as preparation and cite the institution; no license for reproducing exam items was verified.
• Montenegro: https://fcjk.ac.me/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Provjera-znanja-crnogorskoga-jezika-za-strance.pdf is an actual 2018 National Education Council program for permanent-residence candidates, hosted by the Faculty of Montenegrin Language and Literature. I visually inspected all six scanned pages: it specifies at least A2, 90 minutes total, listening 15, reading 30, writing 30 and speaking 15 minutes, 25 points per skill and at least 60% per skill; failing speaking fails the whole assessment. This is a historical published program, not proof of present legal requirements; ask the faculty for current details. The faculty also describes CEFR-aligned A1–B2 courses with placement and semester examinations and certificates at https://fcjk.ac.me/kurs-crnogorskoga-jezika-za-strance/ . Link rather than copy documents without checking rights.
• Croatia: University of Zagreb's Croaticum https://croaticum.ffzg.unizg.hr/?p=7071 is an institutional CEFR assessment resource. Its own navigation links a Croatian language tests page, https://croaticum.ffzg.unizg.hr/?page_id=1062 , but that page returned a 404; no publicly usable Croatian sample exam or reproduction license was verified. Ask Croaticum for the current test materials and recognition.
• Bosnia: International University of Sarajevo offers a Bosnian A1.1 beginner course https://www.ius.edu.ba/en/event/bosnian-language-course-level-a11-0 and A2.1 course https://www.ius.edu.ba/en/event/bosnian-language-course-level-a21 ; they are course alternatives, not verified nationally recognized proficiency examinations. Ask institution about assessment and certificates.
• Council of Europe's CEFR companion descriptors: https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2018/1680787989 . They describe abilities, not an official common four-country vocabulary/exam. No evidence found for a joint embassy test; do not claim one.

SPEECH
• Azure Speech language/voice support https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support identifies sr-RS-SophieNeural and sr-RS-NicholasNeural (Serbian Cyrillic), sr-Latn-RS-SophieNeural and sr-Latn-RS-NicholasNeural (Serbian Latin), hr-HR-GabrijelaNeural and hr-HR-SreckoNeural (Croatian), and bs-BA-VesnaNeural and bs-BA-GoranNeural (Bosnian). Cloud API/SDK and subscription, service terms, region availability and usage charges apply; no local model license. Marked Serbian/Bosnian voices lack phonemes, custom lexicon and visemes according to footnote 3. Serbian Montenegro sr-ME appears in recognition tables, not a distinct listed TTS voice. Voice quality, Serbian input with Montenegrin words and accent need native-speaker audition; I did not audition samples.
• Offline candidate Marko https://huggingface.co/phantom9623/piper-serbian-tts : author model card declares MIT and explicitly warns some prosody needs work; training-data provenance is not fully documented. Download https://huggingface.co/phantom9623/piper-serbian-tts/resolve/main/sr_Marko_medium.onnx and corresponding https://huggingface.co/phantom9623/piper-serbian-tts/resolve/main/sr_Marko_medium.onnx.json . Piper is local ONNX runtime; https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl is GPL-3.0, separate from model terms. Samples available at https://piperhub.org/model/marko-serbian-rs-medium/ ; no listening or accent-quality validation was possible here. Test the actual runtime, script and redistribution licenses before bundling.
• Critical catalog error: Piper's purported sr_RS serbski_institut model card https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/sr/sr_RS/serbski_institut/medium/MODEL_CARD points to https://github.com/marytts/serbski-institut-dsb-data , explicitly Lower Sorbian and CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, despite the sr_RS/Serbian label. dsb/hsb speaker identifiers also indicate Sorbian. Do not present it as a Serbian training voice or infer commercial rights from PiperHub's MIT label.
• eSpeak NG https://github.com/espeak-ng/espeak-ng/blob/master/docs/languages.md lists sr, hr, bs Latin and Cyrillic; no separate Montenegrin voice. GPL-3.0-or-later; offline compact Windows/Linux command-line, library and SAPI options. Its own README https://github.com/espeak-ng/espeak-ng says formant audio is clear but less natural than recording-based synthesis. Sample quality and installed version coverage remain untested.

IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION
Create one curated, sense-based learner core, with separate locale/standard, script, Ekavian/Ijekavian and pronunciation/source metadata rather than labeling every item universal. Start with family, introductions, directions, daily life, food and the four A2 skills, prioritizing Serbian and Montenegrin material. Show Serbian Latin and Cyrillic; make Ekavian/Ijekavian spellings explicit (e.g. mleko/mlijeko), with variants accepted where appropriate, and record country/region-specific lexemes without assuming country borders prescribe a speaker's language. Montenegrin Ijekavian and letters ś/ź deserve optional notes rather than automatic invention; FCJK describes mostly (i)jekavian speech with local variation https://fcjk.ac.me/o-jeziku/ . Have family/native speakers verify translations and audio. First build a small provenance-preserving, manually reviewed pilot; do not import enormous wordlists blindly, claim CEFR levels from frequency, or package copyrighted exam samples without permission.
